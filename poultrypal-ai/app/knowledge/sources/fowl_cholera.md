# Fowl Cholera

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** The bacterium *Pasteurella multocida*.

**Species/age:** Chickens, turkeys, ducks and other poultry; usually older birds (laying age) more than young chicks.

**Key symptoms:**
- Acute form: sudden deaths, sometimes with no prior signs.
- Fever, depression, ruffled feathers, loss of appetite.
- Swollen, dark/purple wattles and face; lameness from swollen joints (chronic form).
- Mucoid discharge from mouth/nostrils; greenish or yellow diarrhoea.

**Transmission:** Bacteria spread via discharges from sick/carrier birds, contaminated water and feed; rodents and wild birds can introduce it.

**Treatment:** Bacterial, so antibiotics can help, especially early. Commonly used options include **sulphonamides**, **tetracyclines** (e.g. **oxytetracycline**), and fluoroquinolones such as **enrofloxacin**; the chronic form responds less well. A veterinarian should select the antibiotic, ideally guided by culture & sensitivity testing, and advise dose and egg/meat withdrawal periods.

**Confirmatory test:** Post-mortem plus bacterial culture & antibiotic sensitivity to confirm *Pasteurella multocida* and select an effective antibiotic.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate sick birds, remove carcasses promptly, control rodents, and disinfect water lines and equipment.

**Prevention:** Rodent control, clean water, biosecurity, and vaccination (bacterins) in problem areas.

**When to call a vet:** Soon to urgently — for confirmation, antibiotic selection, and if sudden deaths are occurring.
