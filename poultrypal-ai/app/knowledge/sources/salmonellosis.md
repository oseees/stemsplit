# Salmonellosis (incl. Pullorum / Fowl Typhoid)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** *Salmonella* bacteria. *S.* Pullorum (pullorum disease) and *S.* Gallinarum (fowl typhoid) are host-specific; others (e.g. *S.* Enteritidis) are zoonotic (food-safety risk).

**Species/age:** Pullorum hits very young chicks hard; fowl typhoid affects growers/adults. Any poultry.

**Key symptoms:**
- Chicks (pullorum): white, pasty diarrhoea stuck around the vent; huddling; high chick mortality; gasping.
- Adults (typhoid): depression, pale/shrunken comb, yellow-green diarrhoea, drop in egg production.
- Sudden deaths can occur; carriers may show no signs.

**Transmission:** Vertical (hen to egg/chick) and horizontal via faeces, contaminated feed/water, equipment, and rodents.

**Treatment:** Antibiotics may reduce mortality but do NOT eliminate carriers, and treating birds destined for food raises residue and public-health concerns. Some antibiotics (e.g. certain **sulphonamides**, and **fluoroquinolones** such as **enrofloxacin**) have been used under veterinary direction and culture & sensitivity. NOTE: nitrofurans (furazolidone) and chloramphenicol were historically used for salmonellosis but are NAFDAC-banned in food poultry — do not use them. In many places the official control for pullorum/typhoid is test-and-removal rather than treatment. Always involve a veterinarian.

**Confirmatory test:** Bacterial culture (faeces or organs at post-mortem) with serotyping and antibiotic sensitivity; serology such as the pullorum rapid plate agglutination test for flock screening.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate, cull persistent carriers per programme, control rodents, disinfect hatchery and housing, and practice good egg hygiene. Handle as a zoonotic/food-safety risk.

**Prevention:** Source certified Salmonella-clean stock, rodent control, hatchery hygiene, vaccination where used, and breeder flock testing.

**When to call a vet:** Soon to urgently — high chick mortality with pasty white vents, or adult deaths with green diarrhoea; also for the test-and-control programme and food-safety guidance.
