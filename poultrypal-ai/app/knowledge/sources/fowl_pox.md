# Fowl Pox

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Avipoxvirus. Two forms — dry (skin) and wet (diphtheritic, in the mouth/throat).

**Species/age:** Chickens, turkeys and other birds, any age. Slow-spreading.

**Key symptoms:**
- Dry form: raised, wart-like scabs on comb, wattles, eyelids and other unfeathered skin.
- Wet form: yellow cheesy lesions in the mouth, throat and windpipe causing difficulty breathing/swallowing.
- Reduced appetite, drop in egg production; mortality usually low unless the wet form blocks airways.

**Transmission:** Spread by biting insects (especially mosquitoes), and through skin wounds or contact with scabs; virus is hardy in the environment.

**Treatment:** No specific antiviral. Supportive care: soft palatable feed, vitamins, gently manage lesions; control secondary bacterial infection on veterinary advice. The wet form is more serious and may need a vet to clear airway lesions.

**Confirmatory test:** Usually diagnosed clinically from the typical scabs/lesions; confirmed if needed by histopathology (Bollinger inclusion bodies) or PCR.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Separate affected birds, control mosquitoes/biting insects, and avoid handling that spreads scabs.

**Prevention:** Vaccination (wing-web stab) in endemic areas; mosquito control; reduce skin injuries.

**When to call a vet:** Soon — especially for the wet/diphtheritic form with breathing difficulty, or to set up vaccination.
