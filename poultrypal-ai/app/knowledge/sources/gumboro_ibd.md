# Infectious Bursal Disease (Gumboro)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV), which attacks the bursa of Fabricius and suppresses the immune system.

**Species/age:** Young chickens, classically 3–6 weeks old. Immunosuppression makes survivors vulnerable to other infections and vaccine failure.

**Key symptoms:**
- Sudden onset, watery/whitish diarrhoea, soiled vent feathers.
- Depression, ruffled feathers, huddling, reluctance to move.
- Vent pecking, dehydration.
- Spiking mortality that rises then falls over a few days.

**Transmission:** Highly contagious via faeces; virus is very hardy and persists in housing. Spread on equipment, people, and litter.

**Treatment:** No specific antiviral. Supportive care only: electrolytes and vitamins in water, warmth, reduce stress, and control secondary infection on veterinary advice. Prevention by vaccination is the real control.

**Confirmatory test:** Post-mortem showing a swollen/haemorrhagic then shrunken (atrophied) bursa of Fabricius; AGID or ELISA serology, or RT-PCR, confirms IBDV.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate affected groups; the virus is extremely persistent, so thorough cleaning and strong disinfection of housing is essential between flocks.

**Prevention:** Vaccination of breeders (for maternal antibody) and chicks per the local programme; strict all-in/all-out hygiene.

**When to call a vet:** Soon — to confirm diagnosis, rule out other causes of high chick mortality, and review the vaccination programme.
