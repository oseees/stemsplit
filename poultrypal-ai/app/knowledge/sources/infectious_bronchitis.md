# Infectious Bronchitis (IB)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Infectious bronchitis virus (a coronavirus of chickens). Highly contagious.

**Species/age:** Chickens of all ages; young chicks show the worst respiratory disease, layers show egg problems.

**Key symptoms:**
- Respiratory: coughing, sneezing, tracheal rales, nasal discharge, watery eyes.
- Layers: sharp drop in egg production; soft-shelled, misshapen, rough or pale eggs; watery egg white.
- Some strains cause kidney damage (wet droppings, increased water intake) or, rarely, gut signs.
- Usually low mortality in adults unless complicated by bacteria.

**Transmission:** Airborne and via respiratory discharges and droppings; spreads very fast through a flock; contaminated equipment and people.

**Treatment:** No specific antiviral. Supportive care: warmth, good ventilation, vitamins and electrolytes. Antibiotics only to control secondary bacterial infection, on veterinary advice. Control is by vaccination.

**Confirmatory test:** RT-PCR and/or virus isolation with strain typing (to choose the matching vaccine); serology (ELISA/HI); post-mortem. A lab test differentiates IB from Newcastle.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate affected groups; the virus spreads readily, so limit movement and disinfect.

**Prevention:** Vaccination matched to local IB strains, good biosecurity, all-in/all-out management.

**When to call a vet:** Soon — to confirm IB versus Newcastle, choose the right vaccine strain, and manage egg-quality losses.
