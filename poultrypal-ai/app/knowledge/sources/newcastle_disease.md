# Newcastle Disease (ND)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Avian paramyxovirus type 1 (a virus). Highly contagious. NOTIFIABLE in Nigeria.

**Species/age:** All ages of chickens; also turkeys, pigeons and many other birds. Unvaccinated flocks are most at risk.

**Key symptoms:**
- Respiratory: gasping, coughing, sneezing, rattling, nasal discharge.
- Nervous (often later): twisted neck (torticollis), paralysis, tremors, circling.
- Greenish watery diarrhoea.
- Sudden drop in egg production; soft-shelled or misshapen eggs.
- High, rapid mortality is common in virulent forms.

**Transmission:** Inhalation/ingestion of virus from respiratory discharges and faeces; contaminated equipment, feed, water, people, and wild birds.

**Treatment:** There is NO specific cure. Care is supportive (vitamins, electrolytes, warmth, reduce stress). Broad-spectrum antibiotics are sometimes used only to control secondary bacterial infection — on veterinary advice. The mainstay is prevention by vaccination, not treatment.

**Confirmatory test:** RT-PCR for NDV (the test of choice) and/or HA-HI serology, plus post-mortem. A national reference lab (e.g. NVRI Vom) confirms and types the virus. Report to government veterinary services first — this is notifiable.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate sick birds immediately; restrict all movement on/off the farm; disinfect footwear, equipment and housing. Report suspected outbreaks to veterinary authorities — this is a notifiable disease.

**Prevention:** Routine vaccination (e.g. Lasota / I-2 / killed vaccines per the local programme), strict biosecurity, control of wild-bird and rodent access.

**When to call a vet:** Urgently — high or rapidly rising mortality, nervous signs, or any suspicion of Newcastle warrants immediate veterinary and regulatory contact.
