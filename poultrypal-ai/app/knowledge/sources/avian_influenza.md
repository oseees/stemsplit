# Avian Influenza (Bird Flu)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Influenza A virus. Low-pathogenic (LPAI) and high-pathogenic (HPAI) forms. NOTIFIABLE; some strains are zoonotic (can infect people).

**Species/age:** All poultry; waterfowl are common reservoirs. Any age.

**Key symptoms:**
- HPAI: sudden death with few signs; very high mortality.
- Swollen head, comb and wattles; purple/blue discolouration (cyanosis) of comb and legs.
- Respiratory distress, nasal discharge, coughing.
- Drop in egg production; soft-shelled eggs.
- Diarrhoea, depression, ruffled feathers, nervous signs.

**Transmission:** Faeces and respiratory secretions of infected birds; contaminated water, equipment, vehicles; wild waterfowl.

**Treatment:** No treatment. Antiviral use in poultry is generally not permitted. Management centres on rapid reporting, containment, and (where mandated) culling under official direction.

**Confirmatory test:** RT-PCR and/or virus isolation at an authorised reference lab (e.g. NVRI Vom); rapid antigen tests for screening. Do NOT delay reporting — the laboratory pathway is directed by veterinary authorities as part of the emergency response.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Immediately isolate; stop all bird and equipment movement; prevent contact with wild birds and standing water; disinfect. **Report immediately to veterinary authorities.** Use personal protective measures because of zoonotic risk.

**Prevention:** Strict biosecurity, separation from waterfowl, vaccination only where officially permitted.

**When to call a vet:** Urgently and immediately — sudden high mortality, swollen/blue heads or combs. Treat as a reportable emergency.
