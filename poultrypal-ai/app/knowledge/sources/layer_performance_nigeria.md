# Layer Egg Production: Targets and How to Improve It (Nigeria)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Figures are typical for good commercial layer strains (e.g. ISA Brown, Lohmann, Bovans) under good management; strain, feed and weather will shift them. Use NAFDAC-registered feeds and check the label for crude protein (CP) and calcium. There is **NO legal hormone or "egg-booster" injection** for hens — production comes from nutrition, light, health and management. Avoid banned drugs and always observe the egg withdrawal period on any medicine.

**Goal:** reach point of lay at **~18–20 weeks**, climb to a peak of **~90–95% hen-day production by ~28–32 weeks**, and hold a long, persistent lay (a good flock lays **~300+ eggs/hen/year**).

**Hen-day production benchmark:** % hen-day = (eggs collected ÷ live hens) × 100. Below ~75% during what should be peak lay signals a problem worth fixing.

**Feed phases (typical):**
- **Chick mash** (0–8 wk): ~20% CP.
- **Grower mash** (8–18 wk): ~16–17% CP — the aim is a uniform, well-framed pullet.
- **Layer mash** (point of lay onward): ~16–18% CP, ~2,750–2,850 kcal/kg ME, and crucially **~3.5–4.5% CALCIUM** with enough phosphorus and methionine. A laying hen eats ~110–125 g/day.

**Safe levers to boost egg production (in order of impact):**
1. **Feed** — feed a proper layer ration with enough energy, protein, methionine and especially **calcium**; provide part of the calcium as coarse oyster shell or limestone for strong shells. Never put a laying flock on grower or broiler feed.
2. **Light** — laying hens need **~16 hours of light per day**; top up with artificial light in the early morning/evening. **NEVER reduce day-length during lay** — it makes birds stop laying.
3. **Water** — cool, clean, unlimited water at all times; even a short water cut can crash production for days.
4. **Heat stress** — temperatures above ~30°C lower feed intake and egg numbers and thin the shells; cool the house, feed in cooler hours, and add vitamins/electrolytes.
5. **Health** — deworm routinely, control external parasites (mites/lice), and vaccinate against egg-drop diseases (Newcastle, infectious bronchitis); these are common production killers.
6. **Body weight and uniformity at point of lay** — under-weight or uneven pullets peak poorly; weigh and grade birds during rearing.
7. **Stress and routine** — keep a calm, consistent routine, enough nest boxes and feeder space, and avoid sudden feed changes (transition gradually over ~5–7 days).

**Common reasons for poor or dropping egg production (Nigeria):** low-energy or low-calcium feed, mouldy/aflatoxin feed, too few light hours, heat stress, water shortage, worms/mites, disease (Newcastle, infectious bronchitis, mycoplasma, egg-drop syndrome), overcrowding, sudden feed change, and an ageing flock past peak.

**Confirmatory check:** record daily eggs and % hen-day. A *sudden* drop with sick birds, odd-shaped or soft-shelled eggs, or respiratory signs points to disease — run a health diagnosis. A *gradual* decline usually points to nutrition, light hours, or flock age.

**When to call a vet/nutritionist:** if good feed, light and management still leave production below the benchmark, have a vet check for disease and a nutritionist assay the feed.
