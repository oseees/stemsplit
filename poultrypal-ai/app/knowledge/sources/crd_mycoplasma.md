# Chronic Respiratory Disease (Mycoplasmosis / CRD)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** *Mycoplasma gallisepticum* (MG), often complicated by E. coli and other respiratory viruses.

**Species/age:** Chickens and turkeys of any age; stress (cold, ammonia, overcrowding, other infections) triggers flare-ups.

**Key symptoms:**
- Persistent respiratory signs: nasal discharge, sneezing, coughing, tracheal rales (rattling).
- Swollen sinuses/face, foamy or watery eyes.
- Reduced appetite and growth; drop in egg production.
- Usually low mortality but lingering, chronic illness and poor performance.

**Transmission:** Bird-to-bird via respiratory secretions; also vertically (hen to egg). Carrier birds remain infected for life.

**Treatment:** Antibiotics active against mycoplasma can reduce signs but do not clear carriers. Commonly used options in Nigeria include **tylosin**, **tiamulin**, **doxycycline**, **tetracyclines** (e.g. **oxytetracycline**), and fluoroquinolones such as **enrofloxacin** — selection, dose and withdrawal periods on veterinary advice, ideally guided by culture & sensitivity. Improve air quality and reduce ammonia and stress alongside any medication.

**Confirmatory test:** Serology (rapid plate agglutination or ELISA) or PCR for *Mycoplasma gallisepticum*; post-mortem plus bacterial culture & antibiotic sensitivity to guide antibiotic choice and rule out E. coli / Newcastle / IB.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Isolate affected birds; because carriers persist, source MG-clean stock and avoid mixing ages/sources.

**Prevention:** MG-free breeding stock, all-in/all-out, good ventilation to limit ammonia, vaccination in some operations.

**When to call a vet:** Soon — for antibiotic choice, to rule out Newcastle/IB, and to plan flock-level control.
