# Marek's Disease

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** A herpesvirus (Marek's disease virus). Causes tumours and paralysis.

**Species/age:** Chickens, usually from about 6 weeks to several months old. Common in unvaccinated flocks.

**Key symptoms:**
- Progressive paralysis of legs or wings — a classic sign is one leg stretched forward and one back.
- Twisted neck, drooping wing.
- Grey/irregular iris or pinpoint pupil; blindness in some birds.
- Weight loss, wasting despite appetite; lumps/tumours on internal organs (seen post-mortem).
- Steady, ongoing mortality rather than a sudden spike.

**Transmission:** Inhalation of virus-laden feather dander and dust; environment stays infective for long periods.

**Treatment:** None. Affected birds do not recover. Control is entirely by vaccination at day old and biosecurity.

**Confirmatory test:** Post-mortem showing tumours and/or thickened peripheral nerves; histopathology and PCR distinguish Marek's from lymphoid leukosis and other causes of paralysis.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Remove affected birds; the dander-borne virus is hard to eliminate, so emphasise day-old vaccination and clean brooding.

**Prevention:** Vaccinate chicks at the hatchery/day old (e.g. HVT-based vaccines); source vaccinated stock; reduce dust and stress.

**When to call a vet:** Soon — to distinguish Marek's from other causes of paralysis (e.g. botulism, injury, nutritional deficiency) and review vaccination.
