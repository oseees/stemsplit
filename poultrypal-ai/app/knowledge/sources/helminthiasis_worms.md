# Intestinal Worms (Helminthiasis)

> ℹ️ **Nigeria context.** Use only NAFDAC-registered products; confirm the exact drug, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period with the product label and a licensed vet. Vaccinate per the National/State programme. Report notifiable diseases (avian influenza, Newcastle) to government veterinary services. NAFDAC bans chloramphenicol, nitrofurans/furazolidone, metronidazole and similar in food poultry — never use them.

**Cause:** Internal parasites — roundworms (*Ascaridia galli*), caecal worms (*Heterakis*), tapeworms, and gapeworms (*Syngamus*, which affect the windpipe).

**Species/age:** All poultry, especially birds with outdoor/range access or on built-up litter. Heavier burdens in older birds.

**Key symptoms:**
- Poor growth, weight loss despite eating; pale comb (anaemia).
- Dull, ruffled feathers; reduced egg production.
- Diarrhoea; worms occasionally visible in droppings.
- Gapeworm: gasping, head-shaking, gaping (stretching the neck to breathe).

**Transmission:** Ingestion of worm eggs from contaminated litter/soil, or via intermediate hosts (earthworms, beetles, snails).

**Treatment:** Deworming with an appropriate anthelmintic. Commonly used dewormers in Nigeria include **piperazine** (roundworms), **levamisole**, **albendazole**, **fenbendazole**, and **flubendazole**. Product choice depends on the worm type and species; a veterinarian should advise the dewormer, dose and egg/meat withdrawal period.

**Confirmatory test:** Faecal egg count (worm eggs seen on flotation) and/or post-mortem showing worms in the gut or trachea (gapeworm). A vet lab can identify the worm type to guide the dewormer.

**Isolation/biosecurity:** Not contagious in the acute sense, but reduce reinfection by rotating range, keeping litter dry, and removing droppings.

**Prevention:** Regular strategic deworming, pasture rotation, dry litter, intermediate-host (beetle/earthworm) control.

**When to call a vet:** Routine to soon — for product choice, especially with persistent poor growth, anaemia, or breathing signs suggesting gapeworm.
